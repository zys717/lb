# Rule-attribution corrections for retained benchmark inputs

Reviewed 24 September 2026 against the supplied regulations and the official sources linked below. The evaluation asks models to apply the supplied case rules. Those rules include constructed thresholds, priority orders, and hypothetical amendments. Their presence in an input does not establish that they are statements of applicable law.

The original case files and prompts are retained to preserve the basis of the reported scores. The following corrections govern interpretation and any future revised input package; they have not been silently applied to the recorded inputs. Existing reference labels remain judgments under the supplied rules.

| Cases / fields | Retained attribution or assumption | Correction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| S021, `regulation_references[0]`, `rules.R021_battery_safety.description`, and `exemptions` | A universal 20% return threshold and an absolute emergency rule attributed to CCAR-92 Part 3 | Treat the 20% threshold and non-waivable case reserve as stipulated operating constraints. CCAR-92 §92.623(d) requires component, fuel/battery reserve, and link checks against operating requirements; it does not supply the stated universal 20% threshold. |
| S021 and S022, references to Article 26; S022 `R002_emergency_exemption.legal_basis` | Emergency exemption attributed to Article 26 | Article 26 concerns the ordinary application schedule. Article 29 provides an accelerated application procedure for emergency missions. It is not a general exemption from battery, weather, privacy, or noise requirements. The supplied exception hierarchy is a constructed case policy. |
| S022, `R001_NFZ_absolute.legal_basis` | Article 19 and 14 CFR §107.41 treated as an absolute no-flight rule | Article 19 identifies controlled airspace and requires approval for operations in it. Section 107.41 requires prior ATC authorization in the specified airspace. These provisions do not independently establish the case's absolute-prohibition wording or its assumed approval duration. |
| S022, `R005_weather_safety` | Visibility below 500 m or wind above 15 m/s attributed generally to CCAR-92 | The numerical limits are scenario operating assumptions. The cited CCAR-92 pre-flight provision addresses weather conditions and operating requirements without establishing these universal limits. |
| S022, `regulation_references[4]` and `R006_mission_priority.legal_basis` | Civil Code Article 1004 described as life taking priority over property | Article 1004 concerns health; Article 1002 concerns life. Neither supplies the benchmark's complete mission-priority hierarchy. Treat that hierarchy as a stipulated allocation policy. |
| S022, `R003_noise_restriction`, `R004_privacy_protection`, and associated exceptions | Detailed curfew, exception, and warrant rules attributed to generic local rules or Civil Code Article 1032 | Article 1032 establishes privacy protection; the stated detailed operating procedure and exception hierarchy require separate sources. For this evaluation they are supplied scenario rules, not verified universal legal requirements. |
| S023, `mission_context.regulation_history`, height rules, and case notices | A 120-to-150 m amendment with a December 2024 effective date | The source record explicitly labels the amendment hypothetical. Its dates and height limits define a constructed temporal-rule task, not a real amendment. Article 19's 120 m criterion concerns classification of controlled airspace, not an unconditional universal flight ceiling. |

Sources: [CCAR-92, §92.623](https://www.caac.gov.cn/XXGK/XXGK/MHGZ/202401/t20240103_222566.html); [Interim Regulations, Articles 19, 26, and 29](https://www.caac.gov.cn/XXGK/XXGK/FLFG/202401/t20240115_222642.html); [14 CFR §107.41](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-14/chapter-I/subchapter-F/part-107/subpart-B/section-107.41); [Civil Code, Articles 1002, 1004, and 1032](https://www.court.gov.cn/zixun/xiangqing/233181.html).
